Freehold vs Leasehold in Malaysia — Which Should You Buy?

By Hani Properties· ERA Real Estate (REN 84031)· 6 min read· Published 18 Feb 2026· Updated 29 Jul 2026

Freehold means you own the land and property permanently, with no expiry. Leasehold means the land is leased from the state, typically for 99 years, after which it reverts to the state unless renewed. Freehold is generally easier to finance and resell; leasehold is more affordable and fine for own-stay if the remaining lease is above 60-70 years.

What is freehold?

A freehold title means you own the land and the property on it permanently — with no expiry date. The government cannot reclaim the land without due process and fair compensation. Freehold properties are generally considered the gold standard in Malaysian real estate.

What is leasehold?

A leasehold title means the land is leased from the state government, typically for 99 years. After the lease expires, ownership reverts to the state — though in practice, leases are almost always renewed. Some older properties may be on 60-year or even 30-year leases.

Which should you choose?

For own-stay, leasehold is perfectly fine — especially if the remaining lease is above 70 years and the location is strong. Most Malaysians live in leasehold properties without issue. For investment or long-term capital gain, freehold gives you more flexibility and a wider buyer pool when you decide to sell.

⚠️ Always check the remaining lease years before buying leasehold. A property with only 45 years remaining will be very difficult to finance and nearly impossible to sell.

How to check if a property is freehold or leasehold

Don't rely on what a listing or agent states verbally — confirm it against the actual title document. You can:

  • Ask the seller's agent for a copy of the issue document of title (geran/hakmilik), which states tenure directly.
  • Do a formal title search at the relevant Land Office / e-Tanah portal for the state the property sits in — this also confirms remaining lease years for leasehold titles.
  • Cross-check tenure against PropertyGuru, EdgeProp or iProperty project listings, which usually state tenure and developer-declared lease start date.

Can leasehold be converted to freehold?

Yes, in some states — but it involves an application to the state land office, a premium payment, and approval is not guaranteed. The process can take years and cost tens of thousands of ringgit. Don't count on it when making a purchase decision.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between freehold and leasehold property in Malaysia?
Freehold means you own the land and property forever with no expiry. Leasehold means the land is leased from the state government, typically for 99 years. Freehold properties are generally easier to sell and finance; leasehold can still be a good investment if the remaining lease is above 60 years.
Is leasehold a bad investment?
Not necessarily. Leasehold with a long remaining lease (70+ years) in a strong location can appreciate well and is much more affordable to enter. It becomes risky mainly once the remaining lease drops below roughly 60 years, when bank financing tightens and buyer demand shrinks.
Do banks lend less for leasehold property?
Banks generally lend the same margin for leasehold as freehold as long as the remaining lease comfortably outlasts the loan tenure (usually 35 years). Margins tighten once remaining lease falls below around 60 years.
