Living in Puchong

Neighbourhoods, transport, amenities and typical prices in one of the Klang Valley's busiest southern suburbs.

Last updated: 16 Oct 2026
Hani Properties›Puchong area guide

Puchong is a large suburb south of Petaling Jaya and Subang Jaya, stretching from Bandar Kinrara in the north to Putra Perdana and Pulau Meranti in the south. It grew quickly along the LDP and is now served by the LRT Sri Petaling Line, with big townships such as Bandar Puteri, Puchong Jaya and Bandar Kinrara. Prices sit between Subang Jaya and the outer suburbs, which makes it popular with families and professionals working in PJ, Subang, Cyberjaya and KL.

Getting around

The LRT Sri Petaling Line runs through Puchong, with stations including Kinrara BK5, IOI Puchong Jaya, Pusat Bandar Puchong, Bandar Puteri, Puchong Perdana and Puchong Prima, ending at Putra Heights, where it meets the Kelana Jaya Line. Northbound trains go through Bukit Jalil and Sri Petaling into central KL.

The LDP is Puchong's main road and gets very busy at peak hours. KESAS, SKVE and the road towards Cyberjaya and Putrajaya give alternatives, which helps if you work south of Puchong.

Neighbourhoods

Bandar Kinrara

Northern Puchong, closest to Bukit Jalil and KL, with an LRT station at Kinrara BK5. A mix of condominiums, apartments and established landed homes.

Puchong Jaya and IOI Mall area

The original commercial heart of Puchong around IOI Mall, with LRT access and plenty of shops and food. Older apartments and terraces at more modest prices.

Bandar Puteri Puchong

A popular township with its own LRT station, commercial centre and a mix of condominiums and landed homes, including some of Puchong's higher-end projects.

Bandar Bukit Puchong and Puchong Perdana

Central and western Puchong, with apartments, condominiums and terrace homes near the LRT and KESAS.

Putra Perdana and Pulau Meranti

Southern Puchong towards Cyberjaya and Putrajaya. Generally the most affordable part of Puchong, with newer townships and lakeside projects.

Daily life

  • Shopping: IOI Mall Puchong, Setiawalk, IOI Boulevard and the commercial centres in Bandar Puteri and Bandar Kinrara.
  • Healthcare: Private hospitals and clinics across the main townships, with Subang Jaya and Bukit Jalil hospitals a short drive away.
  • Education: Plenty of national and private schools, with universities in Subang Jaya, Bukit Jalil and Cyberjaya within driving distance.
  • Work: Puchong's own industrial and commercial areas, plus easy reach of Subang, PJ, Cyberjaya and KL.

Who Puchong suits

  • Families who want established townships with schools and shopping.
  • Professionals working in Subang, PJ, Cyberjaya or KL.
  • LRT commuters who want a direct line into KL.
  • Buyers looking for more space than Subang Jaya at a lower price.

Common questions

How much is rent in Puchong?

From our October 2026 listings, 3-bedroom apartments are about RM1,050 to RM1,600 a month, condominiums and serviced residences RM1,200 to RM2,300, and terrace houses RM2,000 to RM2,300.

Is Puchong connected by LRT?

Yes. The LRT Sri Petaling Line runs through Puchong to Putra Heights, with stations including Kinrara BK5, IOI Puchong Jaya, Bandar Puteri and Puchong Perdana, and continues north into central KL.

Which part of Puchong is most affordable?

Southern Puchong, around Putra Perdana and Pulau Meranti, generally has the lowest prices, with apartments from around RM145k in our listings.

Is traffic bad in Puchong?

The LDP gets congested at peak hours. Living near an LRT station, or using KESAS and SKVE, helps a lot.
