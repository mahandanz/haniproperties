Kuala Lumpur is Malaysia's capital and the centre of the Klang Valley. Around the city centre of KLCC, Bukit Bintang and Brickfields are very different neighbourhoods, from expat-friendly Mont Kiara and Dutamas to family areas such as Wangsa Maju, Setiawangsa and Taman Melawati, and value-for-money pockets along Old Klang Road and Sungai Besi. With the densest rail network in the country, KL suits almost everyone, as long as you pick the right neighbourhood for your budget and commute.

Getting around

KL has the best public transport in Malaysia. Two MRT lines (Kajang and Putrajaya), three LRT lines (Kelana Jaya, Ampang and Sri Petaling), the KL Monorail and KTM Komuter all run through the city, with KL Sentral as the main hub and the ERL from there to KLIA.

For drivers, MRR2 rings the city, DUKE, SMART and the Federal Highway lead into the centre, and MEX heads south to Putrajaya and the airport. Traffic in the centre is heavy, so living near a station makes a big difference.

Neighbourhoods

City centre: KLCC, Bukit Bintang, Pudu and Chow Kit

High-rise condominiums and serviced residences close to offices, malls and nightlife. The most expensive rents in our KL listings, but you can live car-free.

Brickfields and KL Sentral

Around the city's main transport hub, with condominiums and older apartments. Ideal if you travel often or commute by train.

Mont Kiara, Dutamas and Segambut

North-west KL, popular with expatriates and young professionals, with many condominiums, international schools and malls such as Publika.

Wangsa Maju, Setiawangsa, Sri Rampai and Taman Melawati

North-east KL with LRT access, plenty of apartments and established landed homes. Good value for families.

Old Klang Road, Pantai Dalam and Desa Petaling

South and south-west KL, with many affordable apartments and condominiums and quick access to Mid Valley and KL Sentral.

Sungai Besi and Sri Petaling

Southern KL near the MRT and LRT, with affordable condominiums and quick links to Bukit Jalil and Putrajaya.

Daily life

  • Shopping: Suria KLCC, Pavilion KL, Mid Valley Megamall, The Gardens, Publika and many neighbourhood malls.
  • Healthcare: Hospital Kuala Lumpur, plus major private hospitals across the city.
  • Education: Many universities and international schools, especially around Mont Kiara and the city centre.
  • Work: The KLCC, Bukit Bintang, KL Sentral and Mid Valley office districts.

Who Kuala Lumpur suits

  • Working professionals who want to live near the office and commute by train.
  • Expatriates looking at Mont Kiara, Dutamas or the city centre.
  • Families in established neighbourhoods such as Setiawangsa and Taman Melawati.
  • Investors targeting steady rental demand in the capital.

Common questions

How much is rent in Kuala Lumpur?

From our October 2026 listings, most condominiums and serviced residences are RM1,650 to RM4,200 a month, studios RM1,600 to RM2,800, and most apartments RM1,350 to RM2,500. Rents are highest around KLCC and Bukit Bintang.

Which part of KL is most affordable?

Old Klang Road, Pantai Dalam, Desa Petaling, Sungai Besi and Wangsa Maju generally have the most affordable apartments, many of them close to rail stations.

Can I live in KL without a car?

Yes, if you choose a unit near an MRT, LRT, Monorail or KTM station. The city centre, Brickfields, Wangsa Maju and Sri Petaling all have good rail access.
