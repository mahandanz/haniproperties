How to Calculate Rental Deposit in Malaysia (2+1+0.5 Explained)

By Hani Properties· ERA Real Estate (REN 84031)· 5 min read· Published 12 Jan 2026· Updated 29 Jul 2026

In Malaysia, the standard rental deposit is 2+1+0.5 months' rent — 2 months security deposit, 1 month advance rental, and 0.5 month utility deposit, for a total of 3.5 months' rent upfront. On RM1,500/month rent, that's RM5,250 due at signing.

What is the 2+1+0.5 deposit formula?

In Malaysia, landlords typically require three separate payments upfront when you sign a tenancy agreement:

  • 2 months security deposit — held by the landlord as protection against damages or unpaid rent. Refunded at end of tenancy if the unit is in good condition.
  • 1 month advance rental — one month's rent paid upfront, which covers your first month of tenancy.
  • 0.5 month utility deposit — half a month's rent held to cover any unpaid utility bills (electricity, water, internet) at end of tenancy. Refundable.
Total move-in cost formula
(2 × monthly rent) + (1 × monthly rent) + (0.5 × monthly rent) = rent × 3.5
Applies on top of any agent fee or first-month utility setup costs.

Example calculation

Say the monthly rent is RM1,500:

  • Security deposit: RM1,500 × 2 = RM3,000
  • Advance rental (first month): RM1,500 × 1 = RM1,500
  • Utility deposit: RM1,500 × 0.5 = RM750
  • Total upfront: RM5,250

💡 Some landlords negotiate the deposit structure — especially for long-term tenancies (2 years+). Always clarify before signing. The 2+1+0.5 is standard practice, not a legal requirement.

When do you get your deposit back?

Your security deposit and utility deposit should be returned within 30 days after the tenancy ends — provided there are no outstanding bills or damages beyond normal wear and tear. Always do a walkthrough inspection with photos on move-in day to protect yourself, and repeat it at move-out so there's a clear before/after record.

What if the landlord doesn't return the deposit?

If a landlord refuses to return a deposit without valid reason, you can file a claim at the Tribunal for Consumer Claims Malaysia (Tribunal Tuntutan Pengguna Malaysia) for amounts below RM50,000. Filing fee is minimal and no lawyer is required.

Common mistakes tenants make

  • Not photographing the unit's condition on move-in — this is your main evidence if there's a dispute later.
  • Assuming the deposit covers the last month's rent — it doesn't. The security deposit is separate from rent and isn't meant to substitute your final month's payment unless the landlord explicitly agrees.
  • Paying deposit before viewing the unit in person — always view first, or have someone you trust view on your behalf.
  • Not getting the tenancy agreement stamped — an unstamped agreement is harder to enforce if a dispute reaches the Tribunal.

Frequently asked questions

Is rental deposit refundable in Malaysia?
Yes. Both the security deposit and utility deposit are refundable at the end of tenancy, provided there's no unpaid rent, no unpaid utility bills, and no damage beyond normal wear and tear. The advance rental is not refundable since it pays for your first month of stay.
Can a landlord deduct repainting costs from my deposit?
Only if the tenancy agreement specifically states the unit must be repainted on handover, or if walls were damaged beyond normal wear and tear (e.g. large stains, holes). Routine fading from years of normal living is not a valid deduction.
Do I still pay a deposit for room rentals?
Yes, though the amount is usually smaller and less standardised than whole-unit rentals — commonly 1-2 months' room rent as deposit. Always confirm the exact structure with the owner or agent before booking.
